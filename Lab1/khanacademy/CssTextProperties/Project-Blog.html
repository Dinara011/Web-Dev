The Perfectionism Trap: 100 or 0?

Why We Quit When Things Aren't Perfect

12.19.2025

Have you ever missed your early morning alarm and thought, "Well, my whole day is ruined now, I’ll just start again on Monday"? Or maybe you broke your diet with one cookie and decided to eat the whole box because the day was "lost" anyway?
In psychology, this is called "All-or-Nothing Thinking." It’s a perfectionist’s greatest enemy. We operate in only two modes: either a perfect 100 or a total 0. There is no middle ground.
The Problem with 100 vs. 0 When our "ideal plan" gets a tiny scratch, we tend to tear the whole picture apart. If we can’t do a full 1-hour workout, we do zero. If we didn't start work at 9:00 AM sharp, we procrastinate until the next day.

But think about it: if your car gets a flat tire, do you get out and slash the other three? Of course not. You fix the one tire and keep driving. So why do we "slash" our whole day just because one part of it didn't go as planned?

The Solution: Choose "1" over "0" Progress isn't found in perfection; it's found in consistency.

  • Can’t read 50 pages? Read 1 page.
  • Can’t workout for an hour? Do 1 minute of stretching.
  • The morning was unproductive? Make the next hour count.

Do you struggle with the "All-or-Nothing" mindset? How do you get back on track when things go wrong?
